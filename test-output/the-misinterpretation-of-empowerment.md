# The misinterpretation of empowerment

**The Misinterpretation of Empowerment:**


## **Balancing Personal Growth and Responsibility in Relationships**


![image](notion:f82fe111-f9e7-4156-a406-2759d4a6b32a)


In recent years, the rise of social movements emphasizing women’s empowerment, independence, and self-actualization has transformed gender dynamics. Women are increasingly encouraged to pursue personal growth, career ambitions, and autonomy, which are essential steps toward equality and fulfillment. However, like many powerful ideas, these shifts can sometimes be misinterpreted and misapplied—leading not to empowerment, but to imbalance within relationships. This phenomenon mirrors how principles in other areas, such as Islam, have sometimes been distorted to serve individual interests at the expense of the collective good.


At the core of these misinterpretations is a misunderstanding of how independence and personal freedom fit within the framework of partnership. I have personally encountered this imbalance. I was involved with a woman who was focused on her self-actualization: working on her body, developing her interests, and pursuing her goals. While I respected her dedication to her personal growth, the issue arose when she also expected to enjoy all the traditional benefits of being in a relationship without contributing in meaningful ways—financially, emotionally, or in terms of household responsibilities.


This dynamic created a situation where she expected me to assume the traditional role of the provider and emotional leader, while simultaneously detaching herself from any responsibility for my emotional needs or the shared care required to maintain a healthy relationship. This wasn’t just about money; it was about the deeper, often unseen work that makes partnerships thrive. The result was a one-sided arrangement where I was expected to fulfill my role, but she was excused from hers under the premise of prioritizing her individual freedom and growth.


This experience reflects a broader trend in modern relationships, where the message of personal empowerment is sometimes taken out of context. When misapplied, these ideas can foster entitlement—encouraging individuals to prioritize their own interests while avoiding the responsibilities that come with being in a committed relationship. It is not unlike how some aspects of religion, like Islam, have been misinterpreted by individuals for personal gain. In those cases, well-meaning principles, such as discipline or spiritual guidance, are twisted into justifications for oppressive or selfish behaviors. Similarly, the push for independence can, when misunderstood, lead to relational dysfunction rather than mutual growth.


The essence of a healthy relationship lies in balance—where both partners contribute to each other’s well-being in ways that reflect their strengths, and where responsibilities are shared according to what is fair and sustainable. However, when personal fulfillment becomes a justification for avoiding accountability, one partner inevitably carries a disproportionate share of the burden. Over time, this imbalance can erode trust, generate resentment, and weaken the emotional bond between partners.


True empowerment, in the context of relationships, involves more than individual self-fulfillment. It requires recognizing the importance of partnership, where both people actively invest in each other’s well-being. Personal growth should not be used as a reason to disengage from relational responsibilities, but rather as a way to enhance the relationship. When individuals grow within a partnership—supporting each other’s goals and offering emotional care—it creates a dynamic that benefits both partners and strengthens the connection.


The challenge lies in recalibrating these evolving gender roles so that they promote both personal autonomy and relational accountability. Independence and self-actualization should coexist with shared responsibilities and mutual care. Just as religious principles can inspire discipline, guidance, and harmony when correctly applied, the ideas of empowerment and independence can foster healthy, balanced relationships when understood in their full context.


Ultimately, the goal is not to return to rigid traditional roles but to find a new equilibrium where both partners feel valued and supported. Interdependence—where both individuals thrive individually while contributing to a shared life—should be the foundation. This is the key to lasting partnerships: mutual accountability, open communication, and a shared commitment to each other’s growth and happiness.
